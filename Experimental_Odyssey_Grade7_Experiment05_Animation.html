1. What is a chemical change?

A chemical change forms one or more new substances with new properties. Burning magnesium and iron displacing copper from copper sulphate are two examples.

Look for clues such as colour change, heat or light, gas formation, or a new solid.

🧠 Key reactions

Burning magnesium
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO
Bright white light; white magnesium oxide powder.
Ash + water
MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂
Magnesium hydroxide is basic; it turns red litmus blue.
Iron + copper sulphate
Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
Pale green solution and brown copper coating on the nail.
⚠️ Safety first: Burning magnesium gives a dazzling white flame. Never look directly at it; look at the table, not at the flame. Keep flammable materials away. Use tongs and teacher supervision. Copper sulphate is harmful: do not touch or taste it. Dilute sulphuric acid must be handled by the teacher with eye protection.
