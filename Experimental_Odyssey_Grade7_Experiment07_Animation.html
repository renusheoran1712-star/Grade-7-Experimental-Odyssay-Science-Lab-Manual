Heating gives particles energy. The solid ice melts to liquid water.

1. Heating ice

Place a few ice cubes in a heat-safe beaker and warm gently. The ice gradually changes into liquid water.

Melting is a physical change. Water remains water; only its state changes.

🔬 Key learning points

  • Heating can change the state of a solid.
  • Ice and wax melt to form liquids.
  • Some solids, such as camphor, sublime.
  • Different materials have different hardness.
  • Some changes are reversible; others are not.

🧭 Experiment sequence

  1. Heat ice and observe melting.
  2. Warm candle wax and observe melting.
  3. Observe camphor sublimation.
  4. Discuss ammonium chloride vapour and deposition.
  5. Compare soft chalk and hard iron.
  6. Classify reversible and irreversible changes.
⚠️ Safety note: Heating experiments must be performed by a teacher or under close adult supervision. Use heat-resistant glassware, tongs and safety goggles. Never heat a closed container. Camphor and ammonium chloride vapours must not be inhaled; use a fume hood or teacher-approved demonstration. Do not heat unknown substances.
